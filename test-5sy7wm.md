# Coastal Fog and Marine Layers

Coastal fog forms when moist air moves over colder ocean water, cooling until it condenses into suspended droplets that scatter light and soften the horizon. Marine layers often hug the shoreline in the morning, then burn off as the sun warms the land and mixing deepens the boundary layer. This daily rhythm shapes local weather, navigation, and ecosystems along many temperate coasts where the ocean and atmosphere trade heat and moisture.
