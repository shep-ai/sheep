# Morning Fog Over Quiet Harbors

Harbor towns often wake to a thin veil of mist that softens masts, rigging, and still water into a single muted gray plane. The fog muffles everyday sound so that gulls, halyards, and distant engines seem farther away than they really are. By mid-morning the sun usually burns through, leaving ropes beaded with dew and wooden pilings dark with fresh moisture.
