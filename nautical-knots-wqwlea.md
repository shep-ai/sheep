# Nautical Knots Symptom Checker

Use this guide to diagnose problems with common nautical knots.

---

## Is your knot slipping under load?

- **Bowline** — Check that the working end exits inside the loop; if the loop collapses, the tail was too short
- **Cleat hitch** — Ensure the first wrap is a locking turn; slipping means you skipped it
- **Sheet bend** — Both short ends must exit on the same side; opposite sides = slipping

---

## Is your knot jamming and impossible to untie?

| Knot | Likely Cause | Fix |
|------|-------------|-----|
| Reef knot | Pulled with uneven force | Use a bowline instead for critical loads |
| Figure-eight | Loaded while wet | Free with a spike or marlinspike |
| Round turn & two half hitches | Over-tightened under sustained load | Add a stopper or use a rolling hitch |

---

## Does the knot look wrong after tying?

- **Clove hitch looks like a lark's head** — You crossed the wraps in the same direction; reverse the second wrap
- **Bowline loop is twisted** — The bight was formed the wrong way; remake with the rabbit going *up* through the hole
- **Square knot became a granny knot** — Second tuck went the same direction as the first; alternate directions

---

## Is the knot weakening your line?

- Any tight-radius knot (e.g., overhand) reduces line strength by ~40–50%
- Prefer large-radius bends (bowline, figure-eight on a bight) for load-bearing use
- Inspect for glazing or kinking at the knot point — replace the line if present

---

## Quick reference: right knot for the job

| Situation | Recommended Knot |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Loop that won't slip | Bowline |
| Joining two lines of equal diameter | Sheet bend |
| Attaching a line to a post | Clove hitch or round turn & two half hitches |
| Securing to a cleat | Cleat hitch |
| Stopper at end of line | Figure-eight |
